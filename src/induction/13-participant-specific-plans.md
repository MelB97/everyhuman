Every participant is different. The way you provide support must reflect the individual participant's needs, preferences, risks and agreed supports.

Before providing support, make sure you are familiar with the participant-specific information relevant to your role.

Depending on the participant, this may include:

- support instructions and routines
- communication information
- health or medical information relevant to your role
- Behaviour Support Plans
- Mealtime Management Plans
- medication information and procedures
- manual handling or mobility instructions
- seizure or emergency plans
- risk assessments
- personal care instructions
- other participant-specific plans or guidance.

### Follow the Current Plan

Participant-specific plans and instructions exist for a reason.

Do not change, improvise or ignore an instruction because another way seems easier or because “that's how I've always done it.”

If a participant's current needs appear different from the information provided, do not simply change the support yourself.

Observe what is happening, talk with the participant where appropriate, and contact Every Human for guidance.

### When Information Doesn't Match

Sometimes you may find that:

- information is missing
- two instructions appear inconsistent
- another worker or provider tells you something different
- required medication, equipment or support items are not available
- the participant tells you their support has changed
- something simply does not appear right.
Do not guess.

Where the issue may affect the participant's health, safety or support, do not guess or simply proceed. Resolve the discrepancy or contact Every Human for guidance before continuing.

### Participant Choice Still Matters

A support plan guides how support should be provided, but the participant remains at the centre of their support.

Listen to the participant, involve them in decisions and respect their right to make choices about their support.

If a participant declines an aspect of their support, do not force, threaten or pressure them.

Follow the relevant plan or procedure, seek guidance where required, and document significant refusals or changes appropriately.
