Every Human may provide support to older people through aged care, private services or other support arrangements.

Good support for an older person is still person-centred support. Age alone does not determine a person's abilities, interests, preferences or right to be involved in decisions about their own life.

### Respect the Person, Not Their Age

Older people bring a lifetime of experiences, relationships, routines, skills and preferences.

Support Workers should:

- speak directly to the person and involve them in decisions;
- respect their routines, preferences, culture and life experience;
- support independence rather than automatically taking over;
- provide assistance according to what the person actually needs;
- respect privacy, dignity and personal choices; and
- avoid assumptions about what someone can or cannot do because of their age.

Getting older does not automatically mean a person is unable to make their own decisions.

### Providing the Support the Person Needs

Older people receive support for many different reasons. A person may have reduced mobility, an injury, illness, dementia or other health conditions, or may simply require assistance with particular everyday tasks.

Your role is to provide the agreed service respectfully and according to the person's individual needs and preferences.

This may include:

- domestic assistance such as cleaning and household tasks;
- personal care and assistance with showering, dressing or grooming;
- transport and support to attend appointments or activities; or
- other agreed supports.

Do not pressure a person to complete tasks themselves when the purpose of the service is to provide that assistance.

Respect the person's choices about how they would like their support provided, while following their care instructions and staying within your role.

If you notice changes in the person's health, mobility, memory, behaviour, personal care, home environment or ability to manage everyday activities, document what you observed and report relevant concerns to Every Human so they can be communicated to the appropriate Care Manager

or service provider.

### Notice Changes

Older people may experience changes in health, mobility, memory, communication, appetite, mood or their ability to manage everyday activities.

Knowing the person's usual presentation can help you recognise when something has changed.

Observe, document and report significant changes.

Do not diagnose the cause of a change. Record what you actually observed or what the person told you and seek guidance where required.

### Elder Abuse

Older people can experience abuse, neglect or exploitation, including from family members, friends, carers, workers or other people they trust.

Elder abuse may include:

- physical, sexual or psychological abuse;
- neglect;
- financial abuse or exploitation;
- misuse or withholding of medication;
- threats, intimidation or controlling behaviour; or
- preventing a person from accessing their money, belongings, relationships, services or community.

Be alert to unexplained injuries, fearfulness, sudden changes in behaviour, poor hygiene or living conditions, missing money or belongings, unusual financial activity, or another person appearing to control the older person's decisions or access to others.

You do not need to prove that abuse is occurring before raising a concern.

If an older person tells you they are being harmed, neglected, frightened, controlled or financially exploited, listen, take them seriously and report the concern to Every Human.

If anyone is in immediate danger, move to safety without putting yourself at risk and call 000 where required.

Do not investigate the allegation yourself or confront the person suspected of causing the harm.

### Remember

Age does not remove a person's rights, choices or independence.

Support the person. Notice changes. Take concerns seriously. Report when something isn't right.
