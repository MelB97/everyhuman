If you witness, suspect or are told about possible abuse, neglect or exploitation, take the concern seriously.

You do not need to decide whether abuse has occurred and you do not need proof before reporting a concern.

Your responsibility is to respond to any immediate safety concerns without putting yourself at risk, listen to the participant, report the concern to Every Human, and accurately document what you saw, heard or were told.

If anyone is in immediate danger, move to a safe place and call 000 where required. You are not expected to physically intervene or place yourself at risk.

### If a Participant Tells You Something

If a participant tells you that they have been abused, neglected, exploited or harmed:

- remain calm and listen
- take what they tell you seriously
- allow them to speak in their own words
- reassure them that they did the right thing by telling you
- do not blame, judge or express disbelief
- do not ask leading or unnecessary questions
- do not promise to keep the information secret
- explain that you may need to share the information with people who can help keep them safe.

Your role is to listen — not investigate.

### If There Is Immediate Danger

If the participant, you or another person is in immediate danger:

- move to a safe place where possible
- call 000 when emergency assistance is required
- provide reasonable assistance within your role and training
- contact your Area Manager as soon as it is safe to do so.

Your own safety remains a priority. Do not place yourself at risk trying to intervene physically.

### Report the Concern

Any suspected, witnessed or disclosed abuse, neglect or exploitation must be reported to your Area Manager as soon as possible.

This includes concerns involving:

- another Every Human worker
- another service provider
- a family member, friend or carer
- another participant
- any other person.

Do not decide not to report because you are uncertain, because the participant has no visible injury, or because the person involved is someone you know or trust.

Every Human will determine what further action and external reporting may be required.

### Preserve Information

Do not investigate the allegation yourself.

Do not:

- confront the person suspected of causing harm
- interview witnesses
- repeatedly question the participant
- discuss the allegation with workers or other people who do not need to know
- attempt to decide whether the participant's account is true.

Do not alter, delete or dispose of information that may be relevant to the concern. Follow any instructions provided by Every Human, police or emergency services.

### Document What Happened

Record information accurately, objectively and as soon as possible.

Include:

- what you personally saw or heard
- what the participant told you
- relevant dates, times and circumstances
- actions you took
- who you notified.

Where possible, record the participant's own words when documenting a disclosure.

Do not add assumptions, diagnoses or conclusions about what you believe happened.

### Confidentiality

Information about suspected abuse must be treated sensitively and confidentially.

Only share information with people who need it to respond appropriately, including Every Human management, emergency services or other authorised bodies where required.

Confidentiality does not mean keeping abuse secret.

### Remember

**SAFETY — LISTEN — REPORT — DOCUMENT**

You are not expected to investigate or prove that abuse occurred.

If you see something, hear something or are told something that concerns you — report it.
