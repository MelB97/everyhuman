Every Human Support Workers have a duty of care to take reasonable steps to protect participants from foreseeable harm.

At the same time, participants have the right to make choices, take reasonable risks and have control over their own lives. This is known as dignity of risk.

Good support requires you to balance both.

### Duty of Care

Duty of care does not mean preventing participants from doing anything that involves risk.

It means providing support responsibly and taking reasonable action when you become aware of a risk to a participant's health, safety or wellbeing.

This may include:

- following participant-specific plans and instructions
- identifying and reporting hazards or changes in risk
- providing support within your training and role
- responding appropriately to health or safety concerns
- seeking guidance when you are unsure
- escalating serious or immediate risks.

### Dignity of Risk

Participants have the right to make decisions about their own lives — including decisions that may involve some risk.

A participant may choose to:

- try a new activity
- go somewhere independently
- make choices about food or lifestyle
- spend their own money in ways you would not choose
- decline support or assistance
- make decisions you personally disagree with.

Your personal opinion does not override the participant's right to make choices.

Your role is to help the participant understand relevant information and risks, provide the support they need and respect their right to make decisions.

### Choice Does Not Mean Ignoring Risk

Respecting choice and control does not mean ignoring a serious safety concern.

If you believe a participant's decision may place them or another person at significant risk:

- Talk with the participant respectfully about the concern.
- Do not threaten, pressure or simply take control.
- Follow any relevant participant-specific plan or risk-management strategy.
- Contact Every Human for guidance or escalation where required.
- Document significant concerns and actions taken.

If there is an immediate danger or emergency, take reasonable action to protect people from harm and follow Every Human's emergency and incident procedures.

### Remember

Our role is not to remove all risk from a participant's life.

Our role is to support participants to make informed choices, maintain choice and control over their own lives, and manage risks in a reasonable and respectful way.
