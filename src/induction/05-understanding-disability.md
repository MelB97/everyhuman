Disability is different for every person. A person may have one disability or a combination of disabilities, and their support needs may change over time.

Disability may be:

- Physical — affecting movement, mobility, coordination or physical function.
- Intellectual — affecting learning, understanding, problem-solving or everyday skills.
- Neurological — affecting the brain, nervous system, movement, cognition or other functions.
- Sensory — affecting vision, hearing or other sensory processing.
- Psychosocial — where a mental health condition has a significant impact on a person's everyday functioning.
- Cognitive — affecting memory, concentration, processing information, planning or decision-making.
- Developmental — beginning during the developmental period and affecting areas such as communication, learning, behaviour or everyday functioning.

Some disabilities are visible and some are non-visible.

### Diagnosis does not tell you everything

Knowing someone's diagnosis can help you understand some of their support needs, but it does not tell you who they are.

Never assume that everyone with the same diagnosis needs the same support.

For example, two autistic participants may communicate very differently. Two people who use wheelchairs may have completely different levels of mobility and independence. Two people with an intellectual disability may require very different levels of assistance.

Your support must be based on the individual participant, their abilities, preferences, current needs and participant-specific information.

Do not diagnose participants.

You may notice changes in a participant's health, behaviour, mood, mobility or abilities. Your responsibility is to observe, document and report these changes appropriately — not to diagnose the cause.
