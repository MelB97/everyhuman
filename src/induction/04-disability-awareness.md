Disability can affect people in many different ways. Some disabilities are visible, while others may not be immediately apparent.

Two people with the same diagnosis may have completely different abilities, needs, preferences and support requirements.

Never assume what a person can or cannot do because of their disability or diagnosis.

At Every Human, we focus on the person, not the disability.

Get to know each participant and understand:

- how they prefer to communicate
- what they can and want to do independently
- where they would like assistance
- their routines and preferences
- their goals and interests
- any risks or support needs relevant to your role
- any participant-specific plans or instructions you are required to follow
Speak to the participant, not about them.

Do not assume that a person who communicates differently, requires physical assistance or needs support with decision-making cannot understand what is happening around them.

Give people time to communicate and make decisions. Listen, ask questions and adapt your communication to the individual.

### Respectful Support

Always protect the participant's dignity and privacy.

Knock before entering private spaces. Explain what you are doing before providing personal support. Ask permission before touching a person, their mobility equipment or their belongings.

Use respectful, age-appropriate language and follow the participant's preferred name and communication style.

### Communication and Individual Support Needs

Every person communicates differently.

Some participants communicate using speech, while others may use gestures, facial expressions, communication devices, pictures, Auslan, written communication or other methods.

A participant may also need additional time to understand information, process a question or communicate their response.

Do not mistake difficulty communicating for an inability to understand.

When communicating with a participant:

- Speak directly to the participant wherever possible.
- Use their preferred communication method.
- Give them enough time to respond.
- Use clear and respectful language.
- Avoid speaking over the participant or answering for them.
- Check that you have understood them correctly.
- Reduce distractions if this helps the participant communicate.
- Follow any participant-specific communication instructions or plans.
- If a family member, carer or other professional is present, continue to include the participant in conversations about them.

### Support the person — don't take over

Some participants may need additional time, prompting or assistance to make choices or complete everyday activities.

Be patient.

Avoid rushing someone because it would be quicker to do something yourself.

Where appropriate, break tasks or information into smaller steps and allow the participant time to process each step.

Supporting independence does not mean refusing assistance.

The level of support should be based on the individual, what they want to achieve and what they need at that time. Sometimes this may mean doing something for a participant, while at other times it may mean prompting, guiding or supporting them to do it themselves.

### Watch for changes

Getting to know a participant also helps you recognise when something is different.

Changes in communication, behaviour, mood, mobility, appetite, sleep, personal care or usual routines may be important.

Observe, document and report significant changes.

Record what you actually observed or what the participant told you — not your own diagnosis or assumptions about the cause.

### Choice and Control

Participants have the right to make choices about their own lives and supports.

Your role is to provide information, options and assistance where needed — not to make decisions for the participant simply because you believe you know what is best.

A participant may make a different choice from the one you would make. Where there is no immediate safety concern, respect their decision and support their choice.

Where there are safety concerns, explain the concern respectfully and seek guidance where required rather than simply taking control.
