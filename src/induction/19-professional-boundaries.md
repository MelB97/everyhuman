Support Workers often spend significant time with participants and may develop warm, trusting relationships with them.

Building a good relationship is an important part of providing quality support.

However, the relationship remains a professional support relationship.

Professional boundaries protect both the participant and the worker and help ensure that support remains safe, respectful and focused on the participant.

### Be Friendly — But Remain Professional

You can be warm, caring, interested and enjoy spending time with a participant.

Professional boundaries do not mean being cold or distant.

They mean recognising that you are there in your role as an Every Human Support Worker.

Be careful not to allow the relationship to become one where the participant becomes responsible for your emotional needs, personal problems or wellbeing.

### Personal Information

Normal conversation and sharing some appropriate information about yourself can help build rapport.

However, avoid oversharing personal information or discussing matters that may place an inappropriate emotional burden on the participant.

Do not involve participants in:

- your relationship or family problems
- workplace disputes or complaints about other workers
- financial difficulties
- highly personal matters that are not appropriate to the support relationship
- confidential information about another participant.

Participants should never be placed in the position of supporting their Support Worker.

### Personal Contact & Social Media

Use Every Human's approved communication arrangements when communicating with participants.

There may be situations where it is appropriate or necessary for a participant to have your personal mobile number. Before providing your number, consider whether this is appropriate for that participant and the circumstances.

If you are unsure, speak with your Area Manager first. Every Human management can act as the go-between where sharing your personal number is not appropriate or may create a safety or boundary concern.

If you do provide your personal number:

- make it clear that the number is for communication relating to their support
- set boundaries from the beginning
- do not feel obligated to answer calls or messages outside your rostered support time
- redirect ongoing out-of-hours contact through Every Human where appropriate
- tell your Area Manager if contact becomes excessive, inappropriate or makes you uncomfortable.
Do not:

- add or follow participants on personal social media
- communicate privately through personal social-media accounts
- share participant information, photographs or stories on social media
- encourage secret or hidden communication
- share another participant's personal information.

Your personal phone number does not make you available to a participant 24 hours a day.

### Gifts, Money and Favours

Never:

- borrow money from a participant
- lend money to a participant
- ask a participant for money
- use participant funds for yourself
- ask for or encourage gifts
- become involved in a participant's financial affairs outside your authorised support role.

If a participant offers you a gift, money or something of significant value, do not simply accept it. Thank them and contact your Area Manager for guidance.

Be careful with personal favours as well.

Something may feel kind or harmless but can gradually blur the professional relationship.

### Outside Rostered Support

Do not independently arrange:

- unpaid visits
- social outings as a friend
- additional support outside the roster
- private paid work
- overnight stays
- regular contact outside your support role.

If a participant asks you to stay longer, provide additional support or see them outside their rostered support, contact Every Human. Do not make the arrangement directly with the participant.

### Relationships and Sexual Boundaries

Sexual or romantic relationships with participants you support are not appropriate.

Never engage in sexualised behaviour, comments, messages or inappropriate physical contact with a participant.

The NDIS Code of Conduct requires workers to take reasonable steps to prevent and respond to sexual misconduct, and Commission guidance specifically identifies professional boundaries as necessary in relationships between workers and people with disability.

Any sexualised behaviour or sexual misconduct involving a worker and a participant must be reported to Every Human.

### Families, Carers and Other People

Good relationships with families, carers and other providers can be valuable, but your primary responsibility during support is to the participant.

Do not automatically share participant information simply because someone is:

- a parent
- partner
- family member
- friend
- carer
- another Support Worker
- another service provider.

Only share information where it is appropriate and authorised as part of the participant's support or where disclosure is otherwise required.

If you are unsure whether information can be shared, check with Every Human before sharing it.

### Privacy & Confidentiality

Participants have a right to privacy and confidentiality.

Personal information may include:

- health and disability information
- medication information
- behaviour or incident information
- finances
- family circumstances
- photographs or videos
- addresses and contact details
- information about their supports and services.

NDIS guidance makes clear that privacy includes both protecting personal information and respecting a participant's personal boundaries and dignity during support.

### Everyday Privacy Matters

Do not:

- discuss participants where other people can overhear
- discuss one participant with another participant
- share information with workers who do not need it for their role
- leave participant information visible or unsecured
- take screenshots or photographs of confidential information for personal use
- share participant information through unauthorised channels.

When providing personal care, also protect physical privacy and dignity.

Knock before entering private spaces, explain what you are doing and ensure the participant is appropriately covered and comfortable.

### Confidentiality Has Limits

Confidentiality does not mean keeping information secret when someone's safety may be at risk.

Information may need to be reported or shared appropriately when there are concerns involving:

- abuse or neglect
- exploitation
- sexual misconduct
- serious incidents
- child safety
- immediate risks to health or safety
- other legal or reporting obligations.

If a participant asks you to keep something secret that raises a serious safety or safeguarding concern, do not promise secrecy.

Contact your Area Manager and follow the appropriate reporting process.

### When Boundaries Become Unclear

Sometimes there isn't an obvious answer.

If you find yourself thinking - “I'm not sure if this is appropriate...” that is a good reason to stop and ask.

A participant may ask you not to tell Every Human about something that has happened. Respect their privacy wherever possible, but do not agree to withhold information that must be reported because of safety, safeguarding, incidents or your professional responsibilities.

If you are unsure whether information needs to be shared, contact your Area Manager for guidance.

### Remember

Be warm. Be respectful. Be professional.

A good Support Worker can build a genuine and trusting relationship with a participant without becoming part of the participant's personal life.

Protect the participant's privacy, maintain appropriate boundaries and ask for guidance when the line becomes unclear.
