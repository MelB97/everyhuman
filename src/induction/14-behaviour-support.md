Some participants may have a Behaviour Support Plan (BSP) or other participant-specific strategies that explain how they should be supported.

Behaviour is a form of communication. A person's behaviour may be influenced by many things, including their environment, communication needs, health, emotions, sensory needs, relationships, routines or what is happening around them.

Your role is not to diagnose why a behaviour is occurring. Your role is to provide safe, respectful support, follow the participant's plan and report relevant observations.

### Know the Participant's Behaviour Support Plan

If a participant has a Behaviour Support Plan relevant to your role, you are responsible for understanding and following the strategies you are required to use.

This may include:

- known triggers or situations that may increase distress
- early warning signs
- communication strategies
- ways to reduce stress or escalation
- strategies that help the participant feel safe and supported
- what to do during or after an incident
- any reporting or documentation requirements.

Do not wait until a situation escalates before becoming familiar with the plan.

### Positive Behaviour Support

Positive behaviour support is not only about responding when a participant becomes distressed. Good support can help prevent distress and reduce situations that may lead to behaviours of concern.

Get to know what helps the participant feel safe, comfortable and understood.

Depending on the participant, this may include:

- communicating in the way that works best for them
- giving enough time to understand information and respond
- offering meaningful choices and respecting decisions
- maintaining familiar routines where these are important
- preparing the participant for changes or transitions
- recognising early signs of stress, anxiety or frustration
- reducing unnecessary demands or environmental stress
- supporting access to activities, relationships and things that are important to the participant
- recognising sensory needs and providing appropriate space or support
- using encouragement and positive reinforcement where this forms part of the participant's support strategies.

Do not deliberately provoke, test or challenge a participant's behaviour.

If you notice particular situations regularly cause distress, or you discover something that appears to help the participant communicate, regulate or feel safer, document your observations and report them to Every Human. Do not change the participant's Behaviour Support Plan yourself.

### Respond Calmly and Respectfully

If you notice early signs of distress, or a participant's behaviour begins to change:

- remain calm and respectful
- give the person space where appropriate
- use the communication and support strategies in their plan
- reduce demands or environmental stressors where appropriate
- avoid arguing, threatening, challenging or deliberately escalating the situation
- consider your own safety and the safety of others
- seek assistance when required.

Where possible, focus on de-escalation and understanding what the person may be communicating, rather than simply trying to stop the behaviour.

### Your Safety Comes First

The same rule from Health & Safety applies here.

You are never expected to remain in a situation where you feel unsafe.

If behaviour escalates and you feel unsafe, move to a safe place and contact your Area Manager as soon as you are safe.

Call 000 if there is an immediate threat or emergency.

Do not put yourself at risk trying to manage a situation alone.

### Restrictive Practices

A restrictive practice is an intervention that restricts a person's rights or freedom of movement.

Restrictive practices are strictly regulated and must never be introduced or improvised by a Support Worker.

If a participant has an authorised restrictive practice, workers involved must follow the participant's current Behaviour Support Plan and Every Human's instructions, including any training, authorisation, documentation and reporting requirements relevant to their role.

Never create your own restrictive strategy because you believe it will keep someone safe or make their behaviour easier to manage.

This may include physical restraint, restricting a participant's movement or access to certain items or areas, or using medication for the purpose of influencing behaviour. These practices must never be introduced or improvised by a Support Worker. Where a participant has an authorised

restrictive practice, only workers who are appropriately trained and instructed may implement it in accordance with the participant's current Behaviour Support Plan and Every Human's requirements.

### Observe and Report — Don't Label

When documenting behaviour, record what you actually observed.

For example, instead of:

> “John was aggressive.”

record what occurred:

> “John raised his voice, struck the table with an open hand and said, ‘Leave me alone.’”

Include relevant information about what happened before, during and after the behaviour and what support strategies were used.

Avoid judgemental descriptions such as attention seeking, difficult, manipulative, naughty or non-compliant.

### After an Incident

Once everyone is safe:

- check whether anyone has been injured or requires assistance
- follow the participant's plan
- contact Every Human where required
- document what occurred objectively
- complete any required incident or behaviour reporting
- report anything that may require review or follow-up.

Significant changes or patterns in behaviour should also be reported, even when no incident has occurred.

### Remember

Know the plan. Stay calm. Follow the strategies. Don't improvise restrictive practices. Document what actually happened.

And most importantly: if you feel unsafe, get yourself to safety first.
