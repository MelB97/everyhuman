Every Human has a responsibility to help protect participants from abuse, neglect, exploitation and harm.

Abuse can occur anywhere and may involve a worker, family member, friend, another participant, service provider or another person.

Sometimes a participant will tell you directly that something has happened. At other times, you may notice changes, injuries, behaviour or circumstances that cause concern.

You do not need proof of abuse before raising a concern.

### Physical Abuse

Physical abuse involves intentionally causing physical harm or using inappropriate physical force.

Possible indicators may include:

- unexplained or repeated bruises, cuts, burns or injuries
- injuries that do not appear consistent with the explanation provided
- unexplained fractures, sprains or other injuries
- fear or avoidance of a particular person
- sudden changes in behaviour, mood or routine
- unusual withdrawal, distress or anxiety.

### Sexual Abuse

Sexual abuse includes any sexual activity or contact without consent, as well as sexual exploitation.

Possible indicators may include:

- direct or indirect disclosure
- unexplained injuries, pain, bruising or bleeding around intimate areas
- torn or blood-stained clothing or bedding
- unexplained sexually transmitted infection or pregnancy
- sudden changes in behaviour or emotional wellbeing
- unusual fear of a particular person or place
- significant changes in sexual behaviour
- unexplained gifts or money.

### Psychological or Emotional Abuse

This may include intimidation, humiliation, threats, bullying, coercion, controlling behaviour or deliberately causing emotional distress.

Possible indicators may include:

- fear, anxiety or distress around particular people
- unusually low self-esteem or confidence
- withdrawal or significant changes in behaviour
- excessive compliance or appearing afraid to disagree
- being repeatedly threatened, insulted, humiliated or controlled
- self-harming behaviour or significant emotional distress.

### Financial Abuse or Exploitation

Financial abuse involves improperly using or controlling a participant's money, property or financial resources.

Possible indicators may include:

- unexplained missing money or possessions
- unusual withdrawals or purchases
- someone controlling the participant's money without appropriate authority
- persistent missing receipts or unexplained expenditure
- the participant not having enough money for ordinary needs despite having available funds
- pressure to give someone money, property, gifts or access to accounts.

Support Workers must never borrow from, lend money to, or use a participant's funds for themselves.

### Medication Abuse or Misuse

Medication must never be improperly used to punish, control or unnecessarily sedate a participant.

Concerns may include:

- unexplained over-medication or sedation
- prescribed medication being deliberately withheld
- medication being administered differently from authorised instructions
- medication being used to control behaviour without appropriate authorisation.

Medication concerns must be reported and managed according to Every Human's medication and incident procedures.

### Neglect

Neglect occurs when a person's essential care or support needs are not adequately met.

Possible indicators may include:

- persistent hunger, dehydration or inadequate nutrition
- poor hygiene or unmet personal-care needs
- untreated health or dental concerns
- inappropriate or consistently unclean clothing
- unsafe or significantly unhygienic living conditions
- required medication, health care or appointments being repeatedly missed
- essential equipment or supports not being provided
- repeated failure to provide essential or agreed supports without reasonable explanation
- deliberately or repeatedly preventing access to relationships, activities or community participation that are important to the participant

### Restricting Rights or Access

Abuse can also involve deliberately preventing a participant from exercising their rights.

This may include improperly restricting access to:

- their phone or internet
- family, friends or advocates
- money or personal belongings
- health care or other services
- privacy
- complaints or advocacy processes
- legal assistance or other support.

### Look for Changes and Patterns

One indicator does not necessarily mean abuse has occurred.

Pay attention to unexplained changes, repeated concerns, patterns of behaviour and anything a participant tells you.

Your role is not to investigate, interrogate people or decide whether abuse has occurred.

Your role is to:

**NOTICE — LISTEN — RECORD — REPORT**

If a participant tells you something concerning, listen calmly and take what they tell you seriously. Do not interrogate them or ask leading questions.

Record what you actually saw, heard or were told, using objective and respectful language.

If something concerns you, raise it with Every Human even if you are not certain what has happened.
