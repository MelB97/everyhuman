Some participants require high intensity or complex supports because of their health, disability or individual support needs.

These supports may involve greater health and safety risks and may require specific training, competency assessment, participant-specific instruction or clinical oversight.

Examples may include:

- complex bowel care
- enteral feeding and management
- dysphagia and mealtime support
- ventilator support
- tracheostomy support
- urinary catheter support
- subcutaneous injections
- complex wound care
- other complex health-related supports.

This Induction Does Not Authorise You to Provide High Intensity Support

Completing this induction does not mean you are trained, competent or authorised to provide every type of high intensity or complex support.

Before providing a support that requires specific skills or competency, you must have the appropriate:

- training
- participant-specific instruction
- competency or assessment where required
- current support or health plan
- authorisation from Every Human.

Never attempt a complex support simply because you have seen another worker do it before.

### Follow the Participant-Specific Plan

High intensity and complex supports must be provided according to the participant's current individual instructions, plans and procedures.

Do not rely only on memory or on how the support was provided previously.

Before providing the support, make sure you understand:

- what you are required to do
- the correct procedure
- equipment or supplies required
- infection-control requirements
- signs that something may be wrong
- when the support should be stopped
- who you need to contact if there is a concern
- what needs to be documented.

### Stay Within Your Training and Competency

If you are asked to perform something that you:

- have not been trained to do
- have not been assessed as competent to do where required
- do not understand
- have not been authorised to provide
- believe is outside your role

do not attempt it.

Keep the participant safe and contact your Area Manager for guidance.

Never guess, improvise or copy what another worker does.

### If Something Is Different

Sometimes you may arrive for a shift and find that:

- equipment has changed
- supplies are missing
- instructions do not match
- the participant's condition has changed
- another worker or provider gives you different instructions
- something does not appear safe.

Stop and check.

Do not continue with a high intensity or complex support when you are unsure whether it can be provided safely or according to the participant's current plan.

Do not improvise, substitute equipment or supplies, or follow different instructions simply because another worker or provider tells you to.

Contact your Area Manager and seek appropriate guidance before proceeding.

### Recognise Changes and Escalate Concerns

You are not expected to diagnose a medical condition.

You are expected to recognise when something is different from the participant's usual presentation or when something occurs that is identified in their plan as requiring escalation.

Follow the participant's plan and contact the appropriate person or emergency service as required.

If there is an immediate medical emergency, call 000.

### Documentation

Accurately document the support provided and any relevant:

- observations
- changes
- difficulties
- refusals
- equipment or supply issues
- escalation or advice received
- incidents.

Document what actually occurred, not what was expected to occur.

### Remember

**TRAINED — COMPETENT — AUTHORISED — FOLLOW THE PLAN**

If any of those pieces are missing, stop and ask before providing the support.
