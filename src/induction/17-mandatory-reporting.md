Every Human takes all concerns about the safety, wellbeing and rights of participants seriously.

Support Workers have an important responsibility to recognise, respond to and report incidents and concerns.

You are not responsible for deciding whether an incident meets the legal threshold for notification to an external authority.

Your responsibility is to report the incident or concern to Every Human promptly so the appropriate response and reporting requirements can be determined.

### NDIS Reportable Incidents

As a registered NDIS provider, Every Human must notify the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission of certain incidents that occur, or are alleged to have occurred, in connection with the provision of NDIS supports or services.

Reportable incidents include:

- the death of a person with disability
- serious injury of a person with disability
- abuse or neglect of a person with disability
- unlawful sexual or physical contact with, or assault of, a person with disability
- sexual misconduct against, or in the presence of, a person with disability, including grooming for sexual activity
- use of an unauthorised restrictive practice, including a restrictive practice that is not used in accordance with the participant's Behaviour Support Plan or relevant authorisation.

An allegation can be reportable even when it has not yet been proven.

### Reporting Timeframes

Most serious reportable incidents must be notified by the registered provider to the NDIS Commission within 24 hours of the provider becoming aware of the incident.

This includes:

- death
- serious injury
- abuse or neglect
- unlawful sexual or physical contact or assault
- sexual misconduct, including grooming.

An unauthorised restrictive practice that has not caused harm is generally required to be notified within 5 business days. If the unauthorised restrictive practice causes harm, the 24-hour timeframe applies.

These are Every Human's provider reporting deadlines. Workers should not wait for these deadlines — report concerns to your Area Manager as soon as possible.

### What Support Workers Must Do

If you witness, are told about, or become aware of an incident or concern:

- respond to immediate safety needs without putting yourself at risk
- call 000 if there is an immediate threat or emergency
- notify your Area Manager as soon as possible
- follow any participant-specific emergency or safety plans
- do not alter, delete or dispose of information that may be relevant to the incident
- document what you actually saw, heard or were told
- complete any incident documentation required by Every Human
- cooperate with any subsequent investigation or review.
Do not investigate the incident yourself, confront an alleged perpetrator, or decide that something is not serious enough to report.

If you are unsure whether something needs to be reported, contact Every Human and ask.

### Police and Other Authorities

Not every incident is automatically reported to police.

Police should be contacted where there is an emergency, immediate danger, suspected criminal conduct or another legal requirement.

Every Human management is responsible for determining and completing required external notifications, including notification to the NDIS Commission and other relevant authorities.

If there is immediate danger, do not wait for management approval — call 000.

### Children — Additional Mandatory Reporting Requirements

NSW has additional mandatory reporting laws relating to children.

Disability Support Workers and Personal Care Workers who provide services to children as part of their paid work are mandatory reporters in NSW.

Concerns about the safety, welfare or wellbeing of a child must therefore be taken particularly seriously and handled in accordance with Every Human's Child Safety and mandatory reporting procedures.

Do not delay raising a concern with your Area Manager because you are unsure whether the mandatory reporting threshold has been met.

The next section of this induction covers Child Safety in more detail.

### Confidentiality

Information about incidents and allegations must only be shared with people who need the information to respond appropriately.

Do not discuss incidents with other workers, participants, family members or other people simply because they ask what happened.

Every Human will determine who needs to be informed, taking into account the participant's rights, wishes, safety and applicable legal requirements.

### Remember

Workers report concerns to Every Human.

Every Human determines and completes required external reporting.

In an immediate emergency or danger — call 000.

If you are unsure whether something is reportable — report it and ask.
