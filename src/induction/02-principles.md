Our principles guide how we support people every day.

Person-centred support means recognising that each participant is an individual. Their choices, preferences, goals and abilities should guide the support we provide.

Our job is not to take over a person's life. It is to support them to have greater choice, independence and opportunity.

### Choice and Control

Participants have the right to make decisions about their own lives and supports.

As a Support Worker:

- Ask rather than assume.
- Offer choices and explain options.
- Respect a participant's decisions, even when they are different from the choices you would make.
- Encourage the participant to have a voice in how their support is provided.

### Social and Economic Participation

Support should help people participate in ordinary life and pursue the things that are important to them.

This may include friendships, hobbies, education, employment, volunteering, appointments, shopping, attending events or simply becoming more confident in their community.

Look beyond completing today's tasks. Know what the participant is working towards.

### Community Inclusion

Participants should be supported to be part of their community, not simply taken into the community.

Encourage genuine participation, relationships and experiences based on the participant's interests and preferences.

Community access should have purpose and meaning for the person you are supporting.

### Capacity Building

Wherever appropriate, support participants to develop and maintain their own skills rather than automatically doing things for them.

This might mean:

- cooking with someone rather than cooking for them;
- supporting someone to make their own appointment rather than making it for them;
- helping someone learn to use public transport;
- prompting someone through their household routine rather than completing everything yourself; or
- supporting someone to build confidence trying something new.

Sometimes a participant will need you to do something for them. Sometimes they will need encouragement, prompting or assistance to do it themselves.

Good support means knowing the difference.

Participant choice comes first.

Capacity building does not mean forcing someone to participate or refusing assistance because you believe they “should do it themselves”.

Encourage, offer opportunities and provide appropriate support — while respecting the participant's choices, abilities and support needs.
