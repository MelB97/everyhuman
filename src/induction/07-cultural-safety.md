Every Human supports people from many different backgrounds, cultures, communities and life experiences.

A person's culture, identity, beliefs, relationships and experiences may influence what is important to them and how they prefer to receive support.

Every person has the right to feel respected, included and safe to be themselves.

### Respect Individual Identity

Diversity may include differences in:

- culture, race and ethnicity;
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander identity and culture;
- language and communication;
- religion, spirituality and personal beliefs;
- gender and gender identity;
- sexual orientation;
- relationships and family structures;
- age and generation;
- disability and neurodiversity; and
- personal values, traditions and life experiences.

Do not assume that you understand someone's preferences or beliefs simply because you know their cultural background, religion, age, disability or identity.

Ask the person what matters to them.

### Cultural Safety in Everyday Support

Culturally safe support means the participant feels respected and that their identity, beliefs and preferences are not dismissed, judged or overridden.

This may include:

- respecting cultural or religious practices;
- using a person's preferred name, pronouns and communication style;
- respecting food preferences and dietary requirements;
- supporting cultural, community or spiritual activities that are important to them;
- recognising that concepts of family, privacy, personal space, touch and decision-making may differ;
- arranging interpreters or communication support through appropriate channels where required; and
- following participant-specific cultural information or support instructions.

### Your Personal Beliefs Stay Personal

Support Workers will naturally have their own beliefs, values, opinions and experiences.

Your personal beliefs must not determine the choices available to a participant.

Do not pressure a participant to adopt your religious, political, cultural or personal views, and do not criticise or ridicule beliefs, relationships, lifestyles or identities that differ from your own.

You do not have to personally share a participant's beliefs or choices to provide them with respectful support.

### Discrimination and Disrespect

Discriminatory, racist, homophobic, transphobic, sexist, ableist or otherwise degrading comments or behaviour are not acceptable.

If you witness behaviour that may be discriminatory, abusive or harmful to a participant, take the concern seriously and report it through the appropriate Every Human process.

### When You Don't Know

You are not expected to know everything about every culture or identity.

It is better to respectfully ask than to make assumptions.

If you are unsure how to appropriately support a participant's cultural, religious or individual needs, speak with the participant where appropriate and seek guidance from Every Human.

### Remember

Ask, don't assume.

Support the person in front of you — their identity, their beliefs, their relationships, their choices and what matters to them.
