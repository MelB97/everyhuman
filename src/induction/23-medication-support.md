Medication support must always be provided safely, accurately and within the Support Worker's role, training and authorisation.

Different participants require different levels of medication support. Some may manage their own medication, some may require prompting or assistance, and others may have specific medication administration arrangements.

Never assume that because you have supported medication with one participant, the same process applies to another.

### Know Your Role

Before providing medication support, make sure you understand:

- what medication support you are authorised to provide
- the participant's current medication instructions and support arrangements
- where medication is stored
- what documentation is required
- what to do if medication is refused, missing, unavailable or appears incorrect
- who to contact if there is a concern.

Only provide medication support that you are trained, competent and authorised to provide.

If you are unsure, stop and contact Every Human before proceeding.

### Medication Checks

Before assisting with or administering medication, follow the participant's medication instructions and required medication checks.

These may include confirming the appropriate:

- participant
- medication
- dose
- time
- route
- medication record or documentation
- and any other checks required by the participant's medication procedure.

Do not rely on memory, appearance of the medication or what another worker tells you.

Check the current medication information every time.

### Dispensing, Assisting and Taking Medication

Medication support arrangements are different for each participant.

A worker may be required to dispense or provide access to medication at an agreed time, while the participant remains responsible for deciding when to take it in accordance with their individual medication arrangements.

If you dispense or provide medication, you must complete the required medication record at that time. Record exactly what you did.

Dispensing or providing medication does not necessarily mean you witnessed the participant take it.

- If you dispensed or provided the medication, document that it was dispensed or provided.
- If you witnessed the participant take the medication, document this where required.
- If you administered the medication, document it as administered in accordance with the participant's medication procedure.

Never record medication as taken or administered if you did not witness or perform that action.

Some participants may have specific arrangements that allow medication to be dispensed or made available for them to take later, including arrangements designed to reduce risks such as accidental double-dosing. Always follow the participant-specific medication plan.

Where medication has been dispensed for the participant to take later, follow their individual instructions regarding where and how the medication is to be left or stored.

Respect the participant's choice and agreed routine. Do not repeatedly pressure a participant to take medication unless their individual plan requires further prompting or escalation.

### Never Guess or Improvise

Do not:

- change a medication dose
- change the scheduled time without appropriate instruction
- crush, split or alter medication unless authorised
- give medication intended for another time or participant
- substitute medication
- crush, mix or conceal medication in food or drinks unless this is specifically authorised and documented in the participant's medication arrangements
- administer medication because another worker says, “That's what we normally do”
- make a clinical decision about whether medication is needed.

If the medication, instructions or packaging do not match, stop and resolve the discrepancy before proceeding.

### Missing Medication or a Discrepancy

Sometimes medication that should be available may be:

- missing
- damaged
- incorrectly packed
- different from the medication record
- already signed as given
- unavailable when a participant is leaving home
- accompanied by unclear or conflicting instructions.

Do not guess.

If medication is expected to accompany a participant but is missing, do not simply leave because someone says, “They won't need it.”

Resolve the discrepancy with the appropriate person and contact Every Human where required before leaving or proceeding, particularly where the issue could affect the participant's health or safety.

### Medication Refusal

A participant choosing not to take medication immediately is not necessarily refusing medication.

Follow the participant's individual medication arrangements. Some participants may have an agreed period in which they choose when to take medication after it has been dispensed or made available to them.

A medication refusal should be managed according to the participant-specific medication plan and should not be assumed simply because the participant does not take the medication immediately when it is offered.

Participants have the right to make decisions about their own healthcare, including refusing medication.

If a participant refuses medication:

- remain calm and respectful
- do not force, threaten or pressure them
- do not hide medication in food or drink
- explain or offer the medication again only where appropriate
- follow the participant's medication instructions or plan
- contact Every Human or the appropriate health professional where required
- document the refusal and actions taken.

Medication must never be forced.

A refusal may require further action depending on the medication and participant-specific instructions, so do not simply record it and forget about it.

### Medication Errors

A medication error may include situations such as:

- wrong participant
- wrong medication
- wrong dose
- wrong time
- wrong route
- missed medication
- medication given twice
- medication administered contrary to current instructions
- incorrect medication documentation.

If you make or discover a medication error:

Do not hide it or try to fix it yourself.

A medication error or suspected medication error must be reported immediately, even if the participant appears well.

1. Check the participant's immediate wellbeing.

2. Follow participant-specific medication/emergency instructions.

3. Contact Every Human immediately.

4. Seek appropriate medical advice or emergency assistance as directed or required.

5. Call 000 if there is a medical emergency.

6. Complete the required medication and incident documentation.

Report the facts accurately, including what happened, what medication was involved, when it occurred and what actions were taken.

Reporting an error quickly allows the participant to receive the right assistance.

### PRN / “As Required” Medication

Some participants may have medication prescribed to be taken when required (PRN).

PRN medication must only be supported or administered according to the participant's authorised medication instructions and within your training and role.

Follow the participant-specific instructions about when PRN medication may be offered, dispensed or administered and when further advice or escalation is required.

Do not independently decide that PRN medication outside these instructions.

Follow the participant-specific instructions, including the reason for use, required checks, dosage, timing, documentation and any escalation requirements.

Document the medication support you provided at the time, accurately reflecting whether the medication was dispensed, administered or witnessed as taken.

### Medication and Behaviour

Medication must never be used simply to control a participant's behaviour or make them easier to support.

Where medication is used in relation to behaviour and constitutes a chemical restraint, restrictive-practice requirements may apply.

Support Workers must follow the participant's current Behaviour Support Plan, medication instructions and Every Human procedures and must never introduce or alter this practice themselves.

### Documentation

Medication records must be completed accurately and at the required time.

Never:

- sign for medication you did not support or administer
- sign in advance
- record medication as given when it was refused
- alter records to hide an error
- complete another worker's medication documentation.

If you make a documentation mistake, follow the correct process for correcting the record rather than attempting to conceal it.

### Changes in the Participant

Be alert for unexpected changes following medication, such as significant changes in:

- consciousness or alertness
- breathing
- behaviour or confusion
- mobility or coordination
- swallowing
- physical presentation
- other symptoms identified in the participant's plan.

You are not expected to diagnose the cause.

Follow the participant's instructions, contact Every Human and seek appropriate medical assistance where required.

If there is an immediate medical emergency, call 000.

### Remember

**CHECK — SUPPORT SAFELY — DOCUMENT — REPORT CONCERNS**

If something is missing, different, unclear or doesn't look right — STOP AND CHECK.

Never guess with medication.
