Every child and young person has the right to feel safe, be treated with dignity and respect, and be protected from abuse, neglect, exploitation and harm.

Children and young people with disability may rely on other people for communication, personal care, transport, decision-making support or everyday activities. This can sometimes increase their vulnerability to harm.

Every Human is committed to providing supports that are safe, respectful, child-focused and appropriate to the individual child or young person.

### Listen to the Child or Young Person

Children and young people should be involved in decisions about their supports in ways appropriate to their age, communication needs and level of understanding.

Support Workers should:

- speak directly to the child or young person
- listen to their views, preferences and concerns
- use their preferred communication method
- give them time to communicate
- respect their privacy and dignity
- explain what you are doing before providing support
- encourage them to speak up if something makes them uncomfortable
- take concerns and complaints seriously.

Having a disability or communicating differently does not mean a child or young person should be excluded from conversations about their own support.

### Maintain Professional Boundaries

When supporting children and young people, workers must maintain appropriate professional boundaries.

Do not:

- engage in inappropriate physical contact
- make sexualised comments or jokes
- communicate secretly with a child
- develop inappropriate personal relationships
- share inappropriate personal information
- photograph, film or record a child unless authorised and required for their support
- communicate through personal social media or other unauthorised channels
- give inappropriate gifts or encourage the child to keep secrets
- use punishment, humiliation, threats or intimidation.

Support should always be able to withstand professional scrutiny.

### Be Alert to Signs of Harm

Be aware of unexplained injuries, significant changes in behaviour, fear of particular people or places, withdrawal, distress, changes in personal care or presentation, or anything the child or young person tells you.

One sign does not necessarily mean that abuse has occurred.

You do not need to investigate or prove abuse before raising a concern.

### If a Child Tells You Something

If a child or young person tells you they have been harmed or that they do not feel safe:

- remain calm
- listen carefully
- take them seriously
- allow them to use their own words
- do not interrogate them or ask leading questions
- do not promise to keep the information secret
- explain that you may need to tell someone who can help keep them safe
- report the concern promptly.

Where possible, accurately document the child's own words.

### If You Have a Child Safety Concern

If you believe a child or young person may be at risk:

- respond to immediate safety concerns without putting yourself at risk
- call 000 if there is immediate danger or an emergency
- contact your Area Manager as soon as possible
- document what you actually saw, heard or were told
- follow Every Human's reporting procedures
- cooperate with any required follow-up.

Do not investigate the matter yourself or confront the person suspected of causing harm.

### Mandatory Reporting in NSW

Disability Support Workers and Personal Care Workers who provide services to children as part of their paid work are mandatory reporters in NSW.

Where there are reasonable grounds to suspect that a child aged under 16 is at Risk of Significant Harm (ROSH), a mandatory report must be made.

NSW mandatory reporters should use the Mandatory Reporter Guide (MRG) to help determine whether the ROSH threshold is met and whether a report should be made to the NSW Child Protection Helpline on 13 21 11.

Concerns about young people aged 16 or 17 must still be taken seriously and reported to Every Human, even though the same mandatory reporting requirement does not apply.

Support Workers must promptly raise child-safety concerns with their Area Manager. Do not delay raising a concern because you are unsure whether the mandatory reporting threshold has been met.

**If a child or young person is in immediate danger, call 000.**

### Mandatory Reporting in QLD

Queensland has different mandatory reporting laws.

Disability Support Workers are not automatically mandatory reporters under the Queensland Child Protection Act solely because they provide disability support to a child. Certain professions and people working in particular child-care arrangements have specific mandatory reporting

obligations.

However, all adults in Queensland have a legal responsibility to report certain child sexual offending by another adult to police, unless a reasonable excuse applies.

Regardless of the state or whether a particular statutory reporting threshold has been met, Every Human Support Workers must promptly report any concern about the safety of a child or young person to their Area Manager.

If a child or young person is in immediate danger, call 000.

### Working With Children Requirements

Workers undertaking child-related work must hold and maintain the screening and clearances required for their role, including a Working With Children Check (WWCC) where required.

Every Human verifies required WWCC details and other worker screening requirements before allocating workers to relevant roles.

Workers must notify Every Human immediately of anything that may affect their clearance or eligibility to undertake child-related work.

### Children May Be Present During Adult Supports

Support Workers may also come into contact with children even when the participant they are supporting is an adult. For example, a participant may have children, grandchildren or other young family members who live in the home, visit regularly or are present during community activities.

Workers must maintain appropriate professional boundaries and child-safe behaviour whenever children or young people are present, even when the child is not the person receiving the support.

This is also one of the reasons Every Human requires relevant workers to maintain a Working With Children Check (WWCC). It helps ensure our workforce is appropriately screened for the environments in which we provide support.

### Remember

Listen to children. Take concerns seriously. Maintain professional boundaries. Report concerns.

Never assume someone else will report a child-safety concern.

If you are unsure what to do, contact your Area Manager and ask.
